# Oceanman 10K Training Plan

**Event:** Ibiza Oceanman 10K Open Water Swim
**Distance:** 10,000 meters (10K)
**Duration:** 14 weeks
**Target:** Complete comfortably in 150-240 minutes (2:30-4:00)
**Weekly Volume:** 18-30km swimming

## 10K Specific Considerations

### Mental Toughness
**The 10K Challenge:**
- 2.5-4 hours is a LONG time in water
- Mental game is 90% of success
- Must be comfortable with discomfort
- Boredom and monotony are your enemies

**Mental Strategies:**
- Break race into 2km segments (5 segments)
- Count strokes (helps time pass and maintain rhythm)
- Develop mantras: "strong and steady", "smooth and efficient"
- Visualize finish line regularly
- Practice meditation/mindfulness in training

### Nutrition During Race
**Critical for 10K:**
- Must take nutrition every 30-45 minutes
- Dehydration = major performance loss
- Practice in training at feeding stations

**Feeding Strategy:**
- Treading water: 10-20 seconds per feed
- Grab bottle, drink 200-400ml, drop bottle
- Practice quick feeding (time yourself)
- Alternate: sports drink, water, gel

**What to Take:**
- Carb drink (6-8% solution)
- Plain water (alternate feeds)
- Gels/chews if preferred
- Target: 30-60g carbs per hour

### Pacing Strategy
**Conservative Start Critical:**
- First 2km should feel EASY
- Settle into sustainable rhythm by 1km
- Don't get caught up in pack hysteria

**Even Pacing:**
- Aim for even 2km splits
- Monitor effort, not speed (conditions vary)
- Middle 6km = hold steady, don't surge
- Final 2km = maintain or slowly increase

**Expected Pace:**
- Pool pace + 10-15% = open water 10K pace
- Strong amateur: 1:45-2:00 per 100m (2:55-3:20 total)
- Average amateur: 2:00-2:20 per 100m (3:20-3:50 total)
- First-timer: 2:20-2:40 per 100m (3:50-4:25 total)

## Open Water Training

### Minimum Requirements
- 12+ open water training swims
- At least 4x swims over 5km in open water
- 1-2x swims at 8-10km in open water
- Practice feeding at feeding station setup

### Skills to Master
**Sighting:**
- Every 6-10 strokes (more in rough conditions)
- Quick glance, don't disrupt rhythm
- Use multiple landmarks (buoys move!)

**Drafting:**
- Save 10-20% energy by drafting
- Behind feet or on hip of swimmer
- Don't rely on others for navigation
- Be prepared to swim alone

**Conditions:**
- Train in chop, waves, current
- Practice in cold and warm water
- Experience different times of day
- Night swimming if race is early/late

### Course Familiarization
- Swim race course if possible
- Study currents and tides
- Identify landmarks for sighting
- Walk/boat the course to visualize

## Physical Preparation

### Shoulder Health
**Critical for 10K:**
- Shoulder endurance is limiting factor
- Progressive volume increase mandatory
- Never increase volume >10% per week
- Prioritize technique over speed

**Injury Prevention:**
- Proper warm-up every session
- Resistance band exercises 3x/week
- Massage/physio if any soreness
- Ice after long swims if needed

### Core & Posture
- Strong core = better body position
- Do core work 3x per week
- Planks, side planks, dead bugs
- 10-15 minutes per session

### Flexibility
- Shoulder flexibility crucial
- Ankle flexibility for better kick
- Stretch after every swim
- Yoga/mobility 2x per week

## Race Day Execution

### Pre-Race (2-3 hours before)
- Carb-rich breakfast 2.5-3 hours before
- Sip water steadily until 30 min before
- Check water temperature and conditions
- Warm up 15-20 minutes before start
- Apply bodyglide generously
- Mental visualization

### Start Strategy (0-1km)
- Stay calm in chaos
- Breathe steadily, control heart rate
- Don't go out hard - save energy
- Find your rhythm by 500m
- Settle into pace by 1km

### Early Race (1-4km)
- Establish sustainable pace
- Focus on smooth technique
- Practice sighting and navigation
- Take first feed at 30-45 minutes
- Stay mentally engaged

### Mid-Race (4-7km)
- Maintain effort (pace may vary with conditions)
- Feed every 30-45 minutes religiously
- Check form: high elbow, long stroke
- Break into 1km segments mentally
- Use mantras to stay positive

### Late Race (7-10km)
- Form deteriorates - focus on basics
- Don't give up mentally (10K is mental!)
- Keep feeding - dehydration destroys performance
- Final 2km: maintain or gradually increase
- Sprint final 200m to finish

### Difficult Moments
- Everyone suffers - it's part of 10K
- Pain is temporary, DNF is forever
- Focus on process, not time remaining
- Small goals: next buoy, next feed
- Remember your training and preparation

## Recovery

### Immediately Post-Race
- Walk/swim easy 10-15 minutes
- Eat and drink within 30 minutes
- Keep warm (hypothermia risk)
- Don't rush to exit water (orthostatic hypotension)

### Week After Race
- Days 1-3: Rest or easy 1000-2000m
- Days 4-7: Easy 2000-3000m technique
- No hard training for 2 weeks minimum
- Monitor fatigue and motivation

## Coaching Notes

**Who Can Do 10K:**
- Must be comfortable swimming 5K+
- Strong swimming technique essential
- Mental toughness more important than speed
- 6+ months serious swim training minimum

**Time Commitment:**
- 6-8 pool sessions per week
- 2-3 open water sessions per week (build phase)
- Weekly volume: 18-30km
- Biggest time investment of any swimming event

**Common Mistakes:**
- Insufficient volume in base phase
- Going out too fast (ruins entire race)
- Not practicing feeding (leads to dehydration)
- Inadequate open water experience
- Undertrained mentally
- Not completing 10K in training
- Poor shoulder conditioning

**Success Factors:**
1. Complete progressive volume building
2. Swim full 10K distance 1-2x in training
3. Extensive open water practice (12+ swims)
4. Master feeding strategy
5. Mental preparation and toughness
6. Excellent sighting and navigation skills
7. Proper pacing from the gun

## Equipment

**Essential:**
- Well-fitted wetsuit (tested 10+ times)
- Multiple pairs of goggles (clear, tinted, backup)
- Ear plugs (helps prevent vertigo)
- Bodyglide/vaseline (prevent chafing)

**Highly Recommended:**
- GPS swim watch (track distance/pace)
- Bright colored cap (safety/visibility)
- Tow float for training (safety in open water)
- Anti-fog spray for goggles

**For Training:**
- Pull buoy
- Paddles (build shoulder strength)
- Fins (for drills and recovery)
- Snorkel (technique work)

---

**Final Coach's Note:** The 10K open water swim is one of the toughest amateur endurance challenges. Respect the distance. Train consistently. Be patient with progression. The reward of finishing is extraordinary, but the journey requires dedication and mental fortitude. You've got this!
