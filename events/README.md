# Event Training Plans

This folder contains complete, periodized training plans for specific endurance events. Each plan includes phase-by-phase breakdowns, weekly structures, and coaching notes.

## Available Plans

### [Oceanman 5K](oceanman-5k.md)
**Event Type:** Open water swimming
**Distance:** 5,000 meters (5K)
**Duration:** 8 weeks
**Target:** Complete comfortably in 90-150 minutes
**Weekly Volume:** 4-8km swimming (1-2 sessions per week)

**Best for:**
- Amateur swimmers wanting to complete their first 5K
- Time-constrained athletes (minimal training commitment)
- Swimmers stepping up from pool to open water
- Anyone who can swim 2km continuously

**Key Features:**
- Minimalist approach: 1-2 swims per week
- Progressive build to 5km distance
- Essential open water skills (sighting, navigation)
- Realistic for working amateurs
- Proven approach: completable with minimal volume

## How to Use These Plans

### 1. Choose Your Event
Select the plan that matches your goal event. Consider:
- Time commitment (weekly training hours/volume)
- Your current fitness level
- Event date (ensure enough preparation time)
- Previous experience in the sport

### 2. Assess Your Starting Point
**Oceanman 5K Prerequisites:**
- Can swim 2km continuously in pool
- Basic freestyle technique
- Some swimming experience
- No open water experience required

**Oceanman 10K Prerequisites:**
- Can swim 5km continuously
- Strong swimming technique
- Open water experience essential
- 6+ months serious swim training
- Mental toughness and determination

**Mallorca 312 Prerequisites:**
- Comfortable riding 2-3 hours
- Basic cycling fitness (FTP test completed)
- 6+ months of consistent cycling

**Olympic Triathlon Prerequisites:**
- Can swim 1km continuously
- Can bike 60-90 minutes
- Can run 45-60 minutes
- Basic fitness in all three sports

### 3. Follow the Periodization
Each plan is divided into phases:

**Base Phase:**
- Build aerobic foundation
- High volume, lower intensity
- Focus: endurance and consistency

**Build Phase:**
- Add intensity and race-specific work
- Increase volume to peak levels
- Focus: threshold and tempo efforts

**Peak Phase:**
- Maximum training load
- Race simulations
- Focus: confidence and preparation

**Taper Phase:**
- Reduce volume 40-50%
- Maintain intensity
- Focus: rest and recovery

### 4. Adapt to Your Life
**Key Principles:**
- If you miss a workout, skip it and continue
- Don't try to "make up" missed sessions
- If fatigued, replace hard day with easy/recovery
- Life happens - maintain the hard/easy pattern

**When to Adjust:**
- Skip recovery weeks if you're recovering well
- Add extra easy volume if feeling strong
- Reduce intensity if consistently fatigued
- Take extra rest days if getting sick

### 5. Track Your Progress
**Recommended Metrics:**
- Weekly TSS (Training Stress Score)
- Resting heart rate (track daily)
- Workout RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion)
- Sleep quality and quantity
- Body weight (weekly)

**Testing Schedule:**
- Week 1: Baseline fitness tests
- Every 4-6 weeks: Re-test to update zones
- Week before taper: Final test for race zones

## Nutrition Guidelines

### Training Nutrition
**During Short Workouts (<90 min):**
- Water only
- Pre-workout: light carbs 1-2h before

**During Long Workouts (>90 min):**
- 60-90g carbs per hour (cycling)
- 30-60g carbs per hour (running)
- Electrolytes in warm conditions
- Practice race nutrition

**Daily Nutrition:**
- 1.6-2.0g protein per kg bodyweight
- Don't restrict carbs during hard training
- Eat enough to support training load
- Focus on whole foods

### Race Week Nutrition
- No new foods/supplements
- Increase carbs 2-3 days before race
- Stay hydrated (pale yellow urine)
- Light, familiar breakfast on race day

## Recovery Protocols

### Daily Recovery
- 7-9 hours sleep per night
- Afternoon nap if possible (20-30 min)
- Easy days truly easy (conversational pace)
- Complete rest days = NO training

### Weekly Recovery
- Recovery weeks every 3-4 weeks
- Reduce volume 30-40% on recovery weeks
- Maintain some intensity (shorter intervals)
- Extra sleep and nutrition focus

### Signs You Need More Recovery
- Resting HR elevated >10 bpm
- Consistently fatigued for 2+ days
- Motivation completely gone
- Getting sick frequently
- Performance declining despite training

## Coach's Final Notes

**Success Factors:**
1. **Consistency** > Perfection - 80% of workouts at 90% effort beats 50% of workouts at 100%
2. **Recovery** is training - sleep and nutrition are not optional
3. **Listen to your body** - better to undertrain 5% than overtrain 5%
4. **Practice race day** - nutrition, pacing, equipment, transitions
5. **Build confidence** - training should make you feel prepared, not broken

**Common Mistakes:**
- Going too hard on easy days (most common!)
- Not recovering enough between hard efforts
- Skipping recovery weeks
- New equipment/nutrition on race day
- Starting race too fast

**Questions?** These plans are templates. Adjust based on your response to training, life schedule, and individual needs. The best plan is one you can actually complete consistently.
